# Basalt Crown: original bounded scenery enrichment

The existing cooled caldera gains middle-scale geological shoulders and a compact working archive terrace. No route, terrain, road, hero landmark, material palette or gameplay setting is replaced.

## Design

Five unequal broad basalt buttresses frame two upper-rim neighborhoods. Each has an actual terrain-conforming underside, a sloping scree-like lower transition and deep modeled upper cooling joints. Their silhouette supports the original unequal Broken Rim Gate rather than duplicating its teeth or closing the next bend.

The archive extension uses two separate unequal roof masses: a recessed three-bay store wing and an open working portico. Broad grounded aprons, entry steps and a few substantial specimen sections establish a shared purpose. The original archive remains the identifiable hero. The southwest placement follows the real forward-camera bend; the initial northeast proposal would not have worked from the frozen middle view.

The optional inner-bowl bank is omitted. The open basin remains a pacing contrast, and the original radial fan and literal twin-arch bridge remain intact.

## Reference and provenance

Only general near/middle/background scale, meaningful grouped architecture and real negative space were studied from official SEGA kart-racing imagery. No proprietary model, route, texture, logo, UI or distinctive structure is used. Original project geometry and procedural material families supply all emitted assets.

Reference pages: https://sonic.sega.jp/TeamSonicRacing/game/ and https://asia.sega.com/SonicRacingCrossWorlds/en/courses.html

The original source GLB was compared against a fresh untouched-source export. Its complete file hash, mesh/accessor/material records and binary geometry/texture payload matched exactly. The original license and model notices remain authoritative.

## Comparison method

Forty source-native transforms were frozen before geometry edits: road eyes at sampled road +1.6 metres looking 32 metres ahead, and safety-corrected normal view-0 chase cameras. Both use 56-degree vertical field of view and 1280×800 output. The original five catalog transforms remain separately frozen. The very wide upward bridge image is architectural evidence, not a driving view.

Every paired image retains identical original materials, procedural pixels, light, world, exposure, source float32 normal POINT attributes and object-to-world NORMAL shader. Eight-sample drafts test composition only. Full-resolution matched pairs preserve input pixels; contact sheets are explicitly downscaled. CPU static images do not establish native gameplay, GPU usage or FPS.
